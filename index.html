L-1 visas: moving staff from an office abroad

L-1 lets a multinational transfer an employee from its office abroad to its US office: L-1A for managers and executives, L-1B for staff with specialized knowledge of the company. There's no lottery, no annual cap and no wage filing, which is also why far less L-1 data is public. See the Visa guide for how L-1 compares with H-1B.

        Why L-1 data is thinner than H-1B

        • No Department of Labor filing. H-1B employers must file a public Labor Condition Application with the salary and worksite. L-1 has none, so there are no public L-1 salaries or work locations.
        • Employer-level data stopped after FY2019. USCIS published "Approved L-1 Petitions by Employer" for FY2015–FY2019 only. Newer company numbers would need a Freedom of Information Act request.
        • "Blanket L" workers don't show up here. Big multinationals can get a blanket approval, after which each employee applies directly at a US consulate abroad. Those transfers never reach USCIS, so these counts understate L-1 use at large firms, especially IT services companies.
        • Approvals include extensions, not just new transfers. In FY2019, 58% of approved L-1 petitions were extensions, employer changes or amendments, and 42% were new (USCIS).

          How a work-based green card works

          1. PERM labor certification (Department of Labor). The employer proves no qualified US worker is available for the job. The PERM filing date becomes your priority date, your place in line. EB-1 and EB-2 National Interest Waiver skip PERM; their priority date is the I-140 filing date.
          2. I-140 immigrant petition (USCIS). The employer petitions for you in a category: EB-1, EB-2 or EB-3.
          3. Wait for your priority date to be "current" in the monthly Visa Bulletin. Each country can get at most 7% of green cards a year, so people born in India and China wait far longer.
          4. I-485 adjustment of status (or a consular interview abroad). Approved only once your date is before the Final Action Date. You may file earlier using Dates for Filing in months when USCIS allows it.

          Check DOL's current PERM processing times at flag.dol.gov/processingtimes. While you wait, an approved I-140 lets H-1B holders extend past the 6-year limit.

          What the categories mean

          EB-1
          Priority workers: extraordinary ability, outstanding professors and researchers, multinational managers and executives (EB-1C, a common next step from L-1A).
          EB-2
          Advanced degree (master's, or bachelor's + 5 years) or exceptional ability; includes the National Interest Waiver.
          EB-3
          Professionals with a bachelor's degree and skilled workers (2+ years' training). Unskilled "other workers" have a separate line.
          F2A
          Family: spouses and unmarried children under 21 of green card holders.

          US visa types, explained

          A plain-English overview, last reviewed September 2026. Rules change often, so check USCIS or an immigration attorney before relying on this.

          H-1B specialty work

          For jobs that need at least a bachelor's degree in a specific field (software, engineering, finance, medicine…). The employer sponsors you and must pay at least the local prevailing wage.

          • 85,000 new visas a year (65,000 + 20,000 for US master's grads), picked by lottery. Universities and non-profit research employers are exempt from the cap.
          • 3 years + 3-year extension; longer once a green card process is far enough along.
          • You can change employers: the new employer files a petition and you can start once it's filed.
          • Spouse gets H-4; can work only with an H-4 EAD, which requires an approved I-140 or similar.

          L-1A managers

          For managers and executives transferring from a company's office abroad to its US office.

          • You must have worked for the company abroad for 1 continuous year in the last 3.
          • No cap and no lottery.
          • Up to 7 years (1 year at first for a brand-new US office).
          • Fast path to a green card: EB-1C, with no PERM needed.

          L-1B specialized knowledge

          Like L-1A, but for employees with specialized knowledge of the company's products, processes or systems.

          • Same 1-year-abroad rule; no cap, no lottery.
          • Up to 5 years.
          • Green card usually goes through PERM (EB-2/EB-3).

          B-1 / B-2 visitor

          B-1 business: meetings, conferences, negotiating contracts, consulting with colleagues. B-2 tourism: holidays, visiting family, medical treatment. Usually issued together as B-1/B-2.

          • No productive work and no US paycheck, even remotely for a US client.
          • Typically admitted for up to 6 months per visit.
          • Visa Waiver (ESTA) countries can visit up to 90 days without a visa.

          F-1 + OPT students

          Student visa. After graduating you can work on OPT for 12 months, plus 24 more months for STEM degrees. It's the most common route into H-1B.

          O-1 extraordinary ability

          For people with sustained national or international acclaim (awards, publications, high pay, critical roles). No cap, no lottery.

          TN · E-3 · H-1B1 treaty visas

          TN: Canadians and Mexicans in listed professions (USMCA). E-3: Australians, 10,500 a year. H-1B1: Chile (1,400) and Singapore (5,400). All are H-1B-like but have no lottery.

          H-4 · L-2 spouses

          L-2 spouses can work automatically. H-4 spouses need an H-4 EAD, available only once the H-1B holder's green card process reaches an approved I-140 (or certain extensions).

          News & updates

          Immigration rules change often. Here's how to stay on top of them.

          Big changes in 2025–26

          • Wage-weighted H-1B lottery. Starting with FY2027 (registration in March 2026), higher wage levels get more lottery entries. Registrations fell from 343,981 to 211,600. BakerHostetler
          • $100,000 H-1B fee. A September 2025 proclamation required it for new H-1B petitions for people outside the US. A federal court vacated it in June 2026, and the appeals court refused to pause that ruling in July 2026. The case is still going. CUPA-HR · Klasko
